Methodology

1. Accuracy weighting

Each pollster carries a score from their historic accuracy, shown 0–100 in the coloured circle beside their name. A grey circle (50) is a pollster with no track record yet, scored as an average 3-point error.

2. Frequency balancing

So a high-volume pollster can’t dominate the average by sheer quantity, a firm publishing several times inside a 10-day window has its weight shared across those polls.

3. Time-based kernel smoothing (LOESS)

The trendline is a tricube-weighted local average over a 56-day window — wide, because Holyrood polling is sparse enough that a narrow window would let a single poll swing the average. Each vote is smoothed separately and anchored on the actual 7 May 2026 result, so both lines start from a known point.

4. Outlier suppression (bisquare)

A two-pass robust fit: the first pass measures how far each poll sits from the trend, then re-weights it down — to zero for extreme outliers — before fitting again.

5. The Green constituency adjustment

Pollsters ask every respondent how they would vote locally, including in the many seats the Greens do not contest — they stood in six of 73 in 2026. The chart shows what the polls found, but the figure fed to the projection is the Green constituency average divided by three, which is roughly what that question has historically converted to once candidacy is accounted for. No other party is adjusted.
